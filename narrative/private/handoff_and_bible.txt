PART A — APPROVED GAME SPECIFICATIONS (LONG-TERM PRODUCT CONTRACT)
=============================================================================

A1. GAME IDENTITY

- Name: The Hollow Atlas.
- Target: Minecraft Java Edition 1.21.1, Fabric, Java 21; single-player first.
- Genre: original nonlinear open-world fantasy action RPG using Minecraft's
  voxel look but a controlled adventure-map world, not ordinary survival.
- World: fully authored 8,000 x 8,000-block intended final map, around 8–12
  distinctive regions; real cities, towns, roads, forest, mountains, coasts,
  wilderness, secrets, underground authored spaces and natural boundaries.
- Look: vibrant vanilla-plus Minecraft at 32x32 texture resolution. Soft rich
  grass/foliage, warm natural woods, expressive medieval building materials,
  lively but readable palettes. Avoid gratuitous high-poly models.
- Tone: both cozy daily life and intense, emotional adventure; no Zelda copy.
- Player: customizable name/appearance; ordinary nomadic traveler, shared
  unhurried opening, no chosen-one prophecy, no forced home ownership.
- Story: the private narrative bible at the end replaces older premises.
  Nonlinear investigation and a target 30–50-hour *eventual* main campaign.
  The player has asked for surprise: maintain strict spoiler discipline.
- Side content: a broad mix of short tasks, multi-stage quests, discoveries,
  festivals, mysteries, humor, difficult choices and lasting local effects.
- World reacts substantially to significant choices, but the player is not
  constantly rushed by expiry timers. Multiple real faction-driven endings.
- Optional navigation guidance: physical landmarks, clues, tavern rumors,
  journal notes and a living atlas. Do not blanket the map in icons.

A2. WORLD / EXPLORATION CONTRACT

- Final map as above: broad roads, distinct cultures and architecture,
  massive cities plus smaller villages, inns, farms, camps, harbors, dungeons.
- Extremely dense authored secrets and multi-layer clues; find unusual things
  by noticing them, following rumors, exploring old trails and using abilities.
- Unlockable waypoint/travel network. Horses/mount bonding, boats, carriages,
  ships, gliding, stamina-limited climbing, grappling and other traversal.
- Climate and storms affect visibility, routines, hazards and a few puzzles.
- The Atlas tracks rumor vs fact, handwriting/notes, player markers, evolving
  routes and geographic history; map marker precision depends on discoveries.
- Meaningful world changes: bridges reopen, shops expand, routes recover,
  settlements regrow and NPC schedules change. Make changes save-safe.
- Strictly NO random vanilla terrain beyond the authored adventure boundary
  during intended play. Prevent players bypassing map limits or quest puzzles.
- Restricted block breaking/placing everywhere except designated resources,
  quest interactions and approved housing changes. Blocks are not generic loot.

A3. NPC AND LIVING-WORLD CONTRACT

- Detailed schedules: home, work, meals, market, tavern, social time, sleep,
  weather changes, festivals, needs expressed through believable behavior.
- Friendships, rivalries and remembered choices; important personal arcs.
- Handwritten story dialogue + contextual ambient speech. No paid LLM calls
  at runtime. Dialogue choices include investigation, persuasion/intimidation,
  reputation, learned evidence, character history and location/time conditions.
- World events can occur without player involvement: work shifts, caravans,
  merchant restocks, gossip, celebrations and local disputes.
- Truly bustling settlements in final: Tier 1 detailed authored NPCs, Tier 2
  simplified nearby residents, Tier 3 aggregated distant residents. Do NOT
  instantiate thousands of pathfinding entities simultaneously.
- Consequences: witnesses react to crime; guards intervene; merchant access,
  prices, rumors and NPC trust change based on legitimate world state.
- Recruitable companions are BASIC helpers, not a full tactical party manager:
  follow, assist in combat, comment occasionally, survive unloading/reloads.
- Town development is systemic and mostly automatic after the player's
  missions/resources, with meaningful influence over local priorities.

A4. ACTION COMBAT / ENEMIES / DIFFICULTY

- Hardcore action combat, skill-based, with telegraphed precise timing.
- Advanced dodges, perfect blocks/parries, stamina, guard breaks, counters,
  distinct attack styles, class-specific combos and trained weapon mastery.
- Tactical enemies: block, retreat, strafe, telegraph ranged/melee attacks,
  use different patterns, but do not overbuild full group warfare AI.
- Bosses are multi-phase action/puzzle encounters grounded in environments.
- Regional danger plus hybrid scaling within sensible bounds: returning to an
  early area can still feel easier after growth.
- Balanced death: checkpoint recovery, inventory preserved, small transparent
  cost or limited encounter reset (subject to in-game balance tuning).
- Difficulty presets: Story / Normal / Hard / Expert; Hard is closest to
  intended challenge; explain the meaning of each in settings.
- Basic companion fight support only.

A5. ABILITIES AND CHARACTER PROGRESSION

- Eventual 20–30 unique active abilities; most should be trained techniques,
  mobility tools, rare relic mechanisms or physical skills. Magic is extremely
  rare among NPCs and should feel rare for the player as well.
- Unlock a mixture through main story and optional exploration. Branching
  upgrade paths allow meaningful build choice and respecs.
- Magic energy and physical stamina are separate pools; cooldowns for strong
  actions; NO ultimate abilities.
- Abilities affect combat, exploration, secrets, quests, designated structures
  and environmental puzzles. Soft gates, not whole-map hard locks.
- Examples for future integration: Grapple, Windstep, Echo Sight, Reconstruct,
  Blink Dash, Rootbind, Arc Strike, Aegis Guard. The bible defines diegetic
  limits: never turn the protagonist into an ordinary spell-slinging wizard.
- Hybrid progression: levels and trained skills plus quest/exploration rewards;
  chosen weapon techniques and hidden discoveries matter more than grinding.
- Fully customizable NINE-slot hotbar: weapons, equipment actions, consumables
  and abilities in any slot. Select normally and optionally bind direct use
  keys for fast combat. Explain controls and make every binding remappable.

A6. INVENTORY / GEAR / ECONOMY

- Custom category-based RPG inventory (weapons, armor, consumables, materials,
  quest items, valuables) with expandable capacity, readable stats and search.
- Mixed equipment rarity progression and individually memorable weapons with
  distinctive effects; deep blacksmith upgrades keep favorites relevant.
- NO durability on weapons or armor. Never accidentally delete a unique item.
- Specialized crafting (smith, cooking, alchemy, workshops), gathering only at
  designated world resources, no uncontrolled block mining.
- Diverse rewards: unique gear, recipes, clues, materials, maps, decorations;
  collecting is for personal records, NOT mandatory power through set quotas.
- Regional markets have different goods/prices; merchants have personalities,
  restock schedules and personal stories. Keep economy predictable enough that
  losing faction access cannot softlock the story.
- Cooking produces healing and temporary situational bonuses.
- Shared storage within owned homes; owning housing is always optional.

A7. HOUSING, SOCIAL LIFE, OUTDOORS

- Multiple optional purchasable homes with room customization, but the player
  is a traveler. Homes are mostly cosmetic/private; no story lock or huge perks.
- Optional small businesses, not an invasive management simulator.
- No ordinary profession career system; work comes through quests and choices.
- Festivals/events recur; many recreational activities, games, competitions,
  fishing, exploration and wilderness discoveries.
- NPCs have active independent social lives and towns change mood from market
  mornings to tavern evenings to quiet rainy nights.
- Light hunger; sleep completely optional; basic campfires, NOT elaborate tents.
- Regional weather hazards requiring preparation, not full temperature sims.
- Rich species ecosystems with migration, feeding, fleeing and predators,
  simulated efficiently; dangerous humans, wildlife and rare monsters.
- Dynamic travel encounters and meaningful roadside micro-stories, not spam.
- Proper stealth: noise, sight, cover, alerts, distractions and stealth attack.
- Pets mainly cosmetic. Mount ownership, bonding, gear and summon in appropriate
  areas. Followers basic; no heavy companion micromanagement.
- Strong emphasis on outdoor activities, exploration, wildlife observation,
  hunting/fishing challenges and unique discoveries.

A8. USER INTERFACE / AUDIO / GRAPHICS / TECH

- First and third person both fully playable, including combat and dialogue.
- Custom action-RPG controls with optional direct ability shortcuts.
- Fantasy RPG visual interface, detailed information HUD (health/stamina/energy,
  quick access, relevant enemy and quest info, configurable overlays).
- Immersive dialogue UI, portraits or close-up views, occasional framing,
  partial voice acting: short character responses, not every spoken line.
- Dynamic music by region, weather, time, danger and narrative state.
- Reactive audio by environment, crowd, interior, weather and activity.
- Stylized effects and optional shaders; priority is smooth, robust gameplay.
- Target 60 FPS on reasonable gaming hardware once optimized; measure rather
  than claim. Provide crowd, audio, effects and rendering controls.
- Tiered NPC simulation is required for scale.
- RPG saving: autosaves, future manual slots and checkpoints; protect story
  flags and world mutations from corruption.
- Complete packaged adventure: pinned mod versions, bundled world, resource
  pack, dependencies, installer/readme and optional visuals.
- Extensive options: controls, readability, dialogue speed, subtitles, FOV,
  screen effects, HUD display, objective guidance and accessibility.
- REUSE EXISTING COMPATIBLE MODS when they help. Prioritize checking authentic
  Fabric 1.21.1 releases, all dependencies, permissions/licensing, load order,
  multiplayer/client restrictions, mod conflicts and performance. Examples to
  investigate rather than automatically bundle: Sound Physics Remastered,
  Naturalist, wildlife alternatives, camera/movement and optimization tools.
  Some named mods may be Forge-only, unofficial ports, or incompatible:
  NEVER claim compatibility or bundle files without verifying actual releases.

# THE HOLLOW ATLAS

Version: 1.0 — narrative baseline for Minecraft Java 1.21.1 (Fabric)

# 1. Narrative Charter

The Hollow Atlas is a 30–50-hour, nonlinear, single-player fantasy RPG set in an original continent. The protagonist is an ordinary customizable traveler, not an heir, savior, or prophesied vessel. The opening is unhurried and inviting. The primary conflict is a continent-wide market in extremely rare devices called *mercies*—objects that appear to reverse loss, heal harm, or make impossible work possible. The question is not whether magic is evil. The mystery concerns who possesses the legal and material right to decide where the price of relief is paid.

Players should never receive this story bible. The user expressly wants the story, surprises, characters, and endings to remain unknown until played. Public-facing progress updates must use spoiler-neutral engineering language, not plot recaps. All character and quest designs below are provisional names for the game's internal narrative; they may be revised for clarity, but their causality and dramatic functions should be preserved.

Tone: warm everyday life, humorous regional characters, occasional harsh consequences, intelligent political conflict, hard-earned hope. Never flatten all groups into good/evil factions. Magic is extremely rare, dangerous to use casually, and culturally disruptive precisely because most citizens have never encountered it. The player may use 20–30 gameplay abilities in the completed game, but most are martial techniques, mobility devices, and trained skills rather than omnipresent spellcasting. Only a handful are genuinely supernatural.

Narrative principles:
- Every major revelation must be supported by earlier discoverable clues that become meaningful in hindsight.
- The player can investigate three central leads in any order. No secret mandatory clue is attached to a permanently missable quest.
- People are not solely exposition devices. Each major NPC has duties, friends, flaws, ordinary preferences, and personal quests outside the crisis.
- Choices affect civic infrastructure, livelihoods, physical locations, and relationships, with visible postgame states.
- Combat, detective work, travel, and environmental puzzles each matter. Bosses are expressions of characters or systems, not random mythic monsters.
- No generic cosmic apocalypse. The tragedy is that good people may sustain a bad system because their families rely on it.
- Avoid copying the four guardians, ancient calamity, chosen champion, missing town, or reality-rewritten-place storylines.

# 2. Premise — The Measure of Mercy

Within the past decade, a small number of translucent, palm-sized devices known as mercies began circulating through wealthy city hospitals, drought-struck districts, remote mines, and illicit markets. Officially, the devices are recovered relics. They are costly, difficult to operate, often single-purpose, and tracked through paper warrants and stamped copper bands. A mercy cannot create a new object from nothing. It can make one local, physical outcome astonishingly likely: mend tissue, keep a bridge from collapsing for a day, ripen a harvest, calm an overpressured boiler, or filter a reservoir.

The public believes the devices require a harmless 'settling' period. In fact they are linked to a continent-spanning chain of ancient *weighhouses*, half-engineered public works hidden inside canals, aqueducts, caravan stations, mines, and granaries. Each house can distribute the physical fallout of a miracle through a network of designated *receivers*. The original builders intended this network as mutual aid during disasters: large groups of informed communities deliberately consented to share a small, temporary burden so that a catastrophe could be averted in one place. Their mechanisms were not free magic; they were an auditable civic bargain.

Modern devices have been modified with forged seals that allow operators to draw on receivers whose descendants never consented. The harm is diffuse: crop blight, brittle timber, strange animal migrations, dry springs, accidents that seem natural. Importantly, the symptoms are not proof of one simple one-to-one magical transfer. The audience's initial hypothesis that every healing equals another person's injury is wrong. The devices strain local *stability buffers*, which collect many small changes and release them through ordinary physical systems. Badly calibrated draws may damage living systems; well-governed, low-volume use may be sustainable. The first critical moral question is whose consent counts when a voluntary social system spans generations; the second is how to unwind dependence without causing preventable suffering.

There is a stranger layer: the weighhouses were not abandoned by accident. The final assembly in the system, the **Last Assay**, was intentionally divided among six civic authorities to prevent any one city from redirecting the burden. The hardware still exists, but its true operating procedures were fragmented and used as competing political leverage. The catastrophic risk is a scheduled *Reckoning*—a routine of automatic rebalancing that modern operators do not know how to stop, set in motion when accumulated liabilities exceed safeguards. It is not an evil awakening. It is a safety procedure executing without valid recipients, and will sever key waterways and trade junctions unless reconfigured before the next seasonal conjunction.

The primary antagonist is a coalition of interests, habits, and betrayals—not a lone archvillain. A very persuasive human adversary wants to centralize the network under a continental emergency directorate. Their logic is defensible: one governing authority could stop dangerous private misuse tomorrow. Their weakness is the power that centralization would create forever. The player can support regulation, restoration, decentralization, or a negotiated retreat, but no outcome is costless.

# 3. Geography — Ten Regions in an 8,000 × 8,000 Map

The following map regions are diegetic and must match quest coordinates in the final world. These are *worldbuilding targets*, not promises that the map exists already. Each needs large routes, minor settlements, interior spaces, hidden encounters, and a regional identity. The player is a nomad; homes are optional and no main quest requires home ownership.


1. **Westreach Meadows** — starting district; rolling fields, orchard villages, stone culverts, early roadside inns; center: Brindlecross. Safe roads, bandit pockets, modest daily life. Prologue begins here.

2. **Veylport Basin** — river city of markets and guild headquarters; canal barges, warehouses, old floodgates; center: Veylport. First major economic and investigative hub.
3. **The Rainward Canopy** — heavy green forest with living wildlife corridors, abandoned mills, and forest communities; center: Hartroot. Sensitive to environmental strain; stealth and tracking routes.
4. **Garnet Heights** — red-rock terraces, upland farms, reservoirs, and engineers' stations; center: Redbarrow. Heat hazards, waterworks, vantage towers.
5. **Boreal Stair** — cold mountain routes, mining towns, cliff tunnels, ancient assay chambers; center: Sableforge. Climbing, snow, low visibility, mining politics.
6. **Tideglass Reach** — colorful coast, lighthouses, harbors, sea caves, salt gardens; center: Lumehaven. Smuggling and legitimate seaborne trade intertwined.
7. **The Greyfold** — mist-bound peatlands and old causeways; center: Hollowmere. Strong folklore, environmental puzzles, wild animal behavior, misunderstood incidents.
8. **Saffron Expanse** — dry steppe and caravan corridor with oasis settlements and wind-carved ruins; center: Kestrel Rest. Travel, regional shortages, contested toll rights.
9. **The Crownplain** — dense walled capital region, courts, archives and assembly chambers; center: Asterhold. State power and civic law.
10. **The Underways** — limited, authored network of caverns, ventilation shafts and weighhouse service tunnels beneath several regions. Not an unrestricted, globally mineable underground. Major endgame level space, accessed by quests and abilities.

Area design standard: every road should include plausible travel activity. Between hubs place travelers, lodging, market days, animal herds, optional clues, branch trails, and micro-stories. Build secret density through handcrafted sites, not randomly generated chests. The terrain is fixed and protected except quest and resource interactions. Travel is a mix of horse bonding, carriages, ships, unlockable waystations, stamina-limited climbing and gliding, Grapple and other traversal tools. Use the player-facing Atlas to record discoveries and rumors; objective markers are optional.

# 4. Public Factions and Their Private Fractures

Exactly six major faction coalitions shape the campaign. Each can provide quests and economic benefits without automatic membership. Reputation is earned through deeds and evidence. Local chapters can disagree with central leadership.

## F1 — The Brass Exchange
Public: licensed traders and hospital suppliers; reliable transport, price stability, dispute mediation.
Leader: **Maelin Orr**, eloquent director, formerly a field medic who watched a child die because emergency treatment arrived too late.
Virtue: gets relief into hard-to-reach communities. Flaw: normalizes secrecy, tiered pricing, and contractual loopholes.
Internal fracture: Maelin wants regulated access; chief broker **Damon Sile** has falsified receiver warrants and hidden the records. Maelin knows the paperwork is compromised but not the full magnitude.
Gameplay: delivery chains, warehouse infiltration, ledgers, supply-line protection, mercy rationing dilemmas.

## F2 — The Common Assembly
Public: alliance of elected city councils; offers legal legitimacy and public safety codes.
Leader: **Chancellor Ina Morrow**, older negotiator; intelligent and unusually patient.
Virtue: can coordinate repairs and guarantee equitable service. Flaw: emergency powers could become permanent.
Internal fracture: elected delegates debate regional vetoes; a security minister uses a staged attack to push emergency central control.
Gameplay: legal evidence, persuasion checks, hearings, security missions, regional policy outcomes.

## F3 — The Riverwatch Compact
Public: farmers, watershed stewards, ferrymen, rangers and local land associations opposing reckless draws.
Leader: **Seren Fen**, former river engineer who can read environmental symptoms better than anyone.
Virtue: notices losses concealed in official statistics. Flaw: militant wing **the Split Current** targets shipments without regard to dependent hospitals.
Internal fracture: Seren condemns attacks; deputy **Hale Dren** secretly backs a few raids to force investigation.
Gameplay: ecosystem clues, stealth surveillance, water puzzles, protection of vulnerable habitats.

## F4 — The Guild of Assayers
Public: technical scholars and machinists studying unusual materials; operates calibrated labs and repair schools.
Leader: **Master Oris Vale**, deliberate, persuasive advocate of open science.
Virtue: possesses the skills to diagnose and rebuild the network. Flaw: experiments have taken human risks without informed consent.
Internal fracture: junior assayer **Yara Pell** exposed an accident, then was expelled; the guild destroyed its own test results to preserve public trust.
Gameplay: evidence reconstruction, timed machinery puzzles, component testing, skill unlocks.

## F5 — The Stonehand Confederacy
Public: miners, quarry crews, metalworkers, mountain transport cooperatives.
Leader: **Tamsin Rook**, union organizer and brilliant mechanical operator.
Virtue: understands working conditions and the actual material supply. Flaw: closure would destroy livelihoods across mountain communities.
Internal fracture: extraction bosses exploit safety exemptions; workers have been told forged grades are legitimate.
Gameplay: mines, dangerous lifts, camp defense, mechanical bosses, strike and pay disputes.

## F6 — The Unmarked Road
Public: no single public identity; a loose lattice of independent travelers, smuggling crews and unauthorized healers.
Face: **Joss Arden**, cheerful courier who sincerely moves remedies to people excluded from city systems.
Virtue: acts faster than committees and serves marginalized towns. Flaw: smugglers may be carrying counterfeit or dangerously altered mercies.
Internal fracture: cell leader **The Lantern Fox** sells warrants to all sides and later turns out to be several people using one shared title, not a single mastermind.
Gameplay: hidden routes, off-books aid, stealth, disguise options, bargaining and moral choices.

Faction progression: first contact, service, proof of trust, internal dilemma, formal endorsement optional, crisis cooperation/conflict. No faction is always right. Grant multiple ending routes through alliances and evidence rather than a single faction choice UI.

# 6. Supernatural and Mechanical Rules

## 6.1 The mercies
- Devices are scarce (approximately 200–300 confirmed across the continent at campaign start); most villagers have seen none. Many supposed mercies are forgeries or ordinary mechanisms.
- Activation requires compatible material, precise conditions, and a registered (or forged) network seal. Protagonist cannot casually manufacture one.
- Each device has a narrow purpose and local physical effect. Demonstrations are rare and memorable.
- Use settles through receiver buffers. The cost is not direct mirrored injury, nor a sentient curse. It is measurable destabilization in shared environmental and engineered systems.
- Stable use is possible under auditable limits, restoration work, and ongoing consent. Coercive use is the cause of most high-profile harm.
- Publicly known use is limited, contested, and misunderstood; no frequent NPC fireball casting.

## 6.2 Player's abilities and story
- Player's combat abilities are primarily trained martial/mobility skills, devices, and learned techniques. Genuine magic should remain rare and tied to specific finds.
- Echo Sight, Reconstruct, and related supernatural abilities exist as gameplay tools but should be diegetically reframed as uncommon *trace instruments* or skills, not evidence the ordinary traveler is chosen.
- Critical story paths must not require optional abilities. Mandatory story-use abilities are acquired through fair, clear main quest progress or have equivalent nonmagical alternatives.
- Deep world interaction remains: trace clues, reveal repair lines, stabilize a bridge's approved quest blocks, activate mechanisms. Never allow arbitrary terrain destruction.

## 6.3 Why the Last Assay matters
- Six public authorities once countersigned a shared emergency reserve. When private draws exceed safeguards, the Last Assay schedules mechanical isolation of overloaded lines to save the whole lattice. In modern geography, that would close vital canals, spoil food stores, and render trade corridors unusable.
- Isolations are physically plausible regional setbacks rather than instant continent-wide explosion.
- Restoring shared control requires six *attestations* (evidence-backed guarantees from social groups), not six collectible magical crystals. This echoes the player's actual detective work and alliance-building.
- A procedural majority can force central control, but excludes dissenting receivers and entrenches inequity. A federated charter is harder to achieve; a planned shutdown limits future harm but causes painful scarcity.

# 7. Narrative Structure and Pacing

Target: main line 30–50 hours across six acts, depending on exploration, detective depth, combat skill, and which investigation branches are pursued. Optional content is additional. A 30–50-hour target is not a claim that this document alone provides that much authored playtime.

Act I (3–5h): everyday hospitality, early delivery, unsettling local anomaly and first proof. Introduce life before conspiracy; avoid frontloading lore.
Act II (5–7h): port/city markets, three major competing explanations, choice of which witnesses to pursue.
Act III (8–12h): open investigation branches; independent discovery of contracts, ecology, technical apparatus.
Act IV (5–8h): proof of broken consent; faction divisions; legitimate harm caused by well-intentioned actions.
Act V (6–10h): political trial and infrastructure emergency; repair routes and public evidence campaigns.
Act VI (4–8h): Last Assay, negotiable confrontation, final public compact, branching regional outcomes.

Total ~31–50h. Do not force time by filler: use layered interior spaces, voiced snippets only as assets permit, puzzle clarity, variable investigative routes, and NPC routines. No arbitrary compulsory grinding.

# 9. Hard Plot Truths and Reveal Discipline

These are exact facts for writers, not player-facing material.


**Truth A:** Devices are scarce, but the supply has risen because guild refiners found an old technique for repairing dead devices; not because supernatural powers suddenly awakened. Several lots are counterfeits.

**Truth B:** Initial rumors of harm transferred one-for-one are wrong. Impacts accumulate through designated engineered receivers, irrigation, soil moisture, transport materials and ecosystems. The harm follows networks and conditions rather than the exact innocent life supposedly 'paid' for a miracle.

**Truth C:** The original program was consent-based, deliberately limited and widely audited; ancient builders were not all wicked exploiters.

**Truth D:** Modern warrant forgery is systemic, but each faction shares a different responsibility. There are guilty people, not a single omnipotent plotter.

**Truth E:** Oris has hidden dangerous test data, but has also developed a genuinely useful stabilizing method. Bringing him to justice can coexist with using his work.

**Truth F:** The Last Assay is a non-sentient safety machine—not a god, dragon, ancient villain or magic apocalypse.

**Truth G:** Oren's staged incidents accelerate political consolidation. He knows he is breaking the law and believes that an emergency directorate will prevent more deaths. He must remain intelligible even if rejected.

**Truth H:** Community trust is as necessary as technical repair. Player cannot earn ideal ending just by collecting technical documents.

Reveal order: M05 first miracle; M08 real vs false contrast; M10–12 records; two Act III branches expose distributed signatures and physical infrastructure; M14 core mechanism; M16–19 conspiracy details; M20 system failure; M26 countdown; M30 antagonist motive; M32 moral choice. Foreshadow at least three chapters before each reveal through ordinary details (water gauges, stamp marks, recurring phrases, archived measurements). Avoid obvious villain speeches.

# 10. Endings and Conditions

The player must not see the full ending list in the quest journal. Present only supported policy options with world consequences drawn from discovered evidence. Four major ending structures; regional variations depend on earlier decisions.


**Ending E1 — The Public Charter**
Requirements: high evidence quality (>=2 complete branches plus public records), 4+ civic attestation supports, repair work at both critical nodes, no permanently lost key witnesses. Establish a federation of receivers with consent-based draws, transparent audits, limited emergency provisions and independent rights to withdraw. Hospitals keep restricted access; old harms remain and need years of rehabilitation. Trade slows; small towns gain oversight roles. Oren may face tribunal or become a supervised technical adviser depending on confrontation outcome.


**Ending E2 — The Emergency Directorate**
Requirements: Common Assembly support OR technical emergency clause. Centralize network authority under a reviewable but initially broad emergency office. Stabilizes high-volume hospital and water uses rapidly. Some vulnerable regions remain obligated; dissenting communities have limited vetoes. Cities prosper quickly, and rural resentment rises. This is politically effective but ethically uneasy. Postgame includes oversight and resistance quests.


**Ending E3 — The Gentle Closure**
Requirements: specific infrastructure study and sufficient alternate supply chain work OR acceptance of greater short-term hardship. Gradually decommission primary draw network while reserving a finite, regulated emergency stock. Many damaged habitats recover, but hospitals lose a lifesaving tool; the player should see transition programs and communities refusing a simple moral verdict. Economic upheaval and retraining quests continue postgame.


**Ending E4 — The Distributed Compact**
Requirements: strong relationships with at least four community-level groups, discoveries from all three investigation branches, protected witnesses and repaired understations. Rebuild decentralized local weighhouses whose communities decide participation; lower maximum capacity and slower response than centralization, but greater autonomy. The main downside is uneven protection across regions and operational fragility. Ending must not be framed as objectively perfect.


**Emergency fallback — A Winter Without Balance**
Available if player ignored most evidence or made destructive choices. The network is partially isolated to avert immediate catastrophe. Suffering is regional but not annihilation. Player continues exploring and rebuilding, with a damaged-travel-world epilogue. Treat as a valid difficult outcome, not a 'game over'.

Regional epilogues independently track: `riverwatch_treaty`, `mine_safety_reform`, `witness_protected`, `supply_route_open`, `orchard_restored`, `smugglers_legalized`, `brindlecross_prosperity`, `last_assay_policy`, `dask_disposition`. Use these flags to select modest changes to world assets, shops, encounters and NPC dialogue.

# 12. Puzzle Dungeons and Boss Design

Story dungeons are authored areas with immutable scenery and limited puzzle interactables. Every puzzle should show affordances, state, reset logic and accessibility alternatives. Never make arbitrary mining solve them.

D1 **Floodgate Vault** (Act IV): rotating water channels, pressure gauges, register-reading and vertical climbing. Boss-like encounter: a hydraulic lock assembly, telegraphing steam bursts and shifting platforms.
D2 **Breathing Lift** (Act III Mountain): descent timing, counterweights and narrow service bridges. Major hazard challenge; optional mechanical miniboss.
D3 **The Sealed Archive** (Act III Civic): investigation dungeon with witness statements, calendar contradictions, mechanical locks and quiet stealth sections, no required combat.
D4 **The River's Teeth** (Act III River): wildlife migration paths, floodplain terrain, sluice control, bridge repair; boss is a defensive altered marsh predator with the option of nonlethal disengagement.
D5 **Saltglass Catacombs** (Act V): smugglers' paths, decoy cargo and dual-route stealth/combat infiltration, environmental trap puzzle.
D6 **The Redbarrow Regulators** (Act V): timed valves, wind pressure, multi-level mining/repair routes and a heavily armored human enforcer boss with telegraphed guard breaks.
D7 **Asterhold Service Court** (Act V): social-infiltration space with schedules, permission slips, evidence and quiet traversal.
D8 **The Last Assay** (Act VI): three-stage final environmental dungeon featuring changing conduit networks, consent-credential puzzle and a physical isolation race. Finale combat against Oren if diplomacy fails; no giant ancient demon.

Hardcore action combat: weapons have distinct stances, dodges, guard-breaks, stamina management, and skill rewards. Bosses have multiple readable phases, puzzles, and nonmagical environmental solutions; they should not assume the player has optional rare magic.

# 13. Dialogue and Detective Systems

All dialogue is authored and localized; use contextual lines for time, weather, relationship and world state. No mandatory paid AI. Each conversation offers characterful responses but never makes the player guess an arbitrary correct line to avoid losing the game.

Evidence model: collectible evidence entries with source, confidence, location, timestamp/order marker, witness reliability, contradiction sets, and case-board links. Deduction screen lets player connect claims and evidence. Incorrect hypotheses produce believable, recoverable reactions and may change optional NPC trust; the player can revisit with new evidence.

Key dice-free dialogue checks: `knowledge` (evidence learned), `reputation` (faction/local), `relationship` (individual), `background` (ordinary traveler flavor), `skill` (non-exclusive skill thresholds), `time/location`, `quest_state`. Never require extensive early grinding to pass critical check. Player's name/appearance customizable; backstory is simply an ordinary traveler. No prophecy.

Conversation style: concise ordinary speech with occasional humor, no constant exposition or encyclopedic monologues. Merchants discuss daily life; children have safe age-appropriate incidental lines; people hold genuine disagreement without sounding like philosophical essays.

The side quest log distinguishes player-observed fact, tentative deduction and rumor. The living Atlas pins visited sites and maps changing trade routes. When an NPC mentions a rumored location, it appears as an approximate area, not an exact marker by default; optional guidance can be enabled.

# 14. World Life and System Integration

- Major cities have dense visible crowds, but not thousands of fully simulated live entities. Use tiered simulation: authored high-detail NPCs, local scheduled residents, distant aggregate population. Maintain deterministic routines and safe state persistence.
- NPCs socialize, gossip, celebrate recurring holidays, visit work, move to taverns, react to weather and witness crimes. Faction choices affect shops and travel, not just dialogue.
- Player is primarily nomadic: inns and optional campfires support travel. Owned houses are cosmetic/private, optional multiple purchases; no housing lock on crafting or story progression. Shared home storage if a home is purchased.
- Periodic roadside encounters include neutral traders, lost travelers, animal migration, weather events, guards, smugglers, and small personal stories. No excessive ambush spam.
- Very rich wildlife is possible through version-compatible licensed mods where appropriate. Verify Fabric 1.21.1, dependency versions, licenses, and performance. Existing audio/ecosystem mods should be integrated where safe rather than rewritten.
- Light hunger, optional sleep, regional weather hazards, cosmetic pets, bondable mounts, simple companions, and proper stealth. Campfires only, not a full tent-management system.
- No weapon or armor durability. Use mixed rare equipment and unique effects, deep upgrades, categorized inventory, expandable storage, specialized crafting, regional economy, memorable loot, personal-only collections, and fully customizable nine-slot weapons/abilities bar. No ultimates. Abilities have separate stamina/energy and branching upgrades, 20–30 total, with soft gating.
- Support first and third person, custom action controls, detailed HUD, partial voice acting, dynamic soundtrack, reactive ambience and optional effects; stable performance comes first. All optional external mods vetted for license and version before shipping.

# 15. Spoiler Containment in Engineering Workflow

This section is a strict production requirement from the player, not flavor.

1. Private plot docs stay in `narrative/private/` and are never included in game launcher announcements, public changelogs or player-readable repository summary.
2. Use opaque quest IDs (M01, R03, etc.) in developer progress displays. Avoid secret names and plot descriptions in screenshots, filenames, window titles and commit messages that may be seen by the player.
3. Any test/demo quest shown to the player must be from a **spoiler-free sandbox** with invented disposable NPCs rather than a main-plot event.
4. If explaining a bug requires spoilers, describe it generically ('Act IV case-board transition') rather than giving away the revelation.
5. Final in-game quest content naturally reveals as player progress unlocks it; content gating is a gameplay behavior, not a security feature. A user who opens the shipped resource files could read scripts. Be accurate about this limitation.
6. Deliver narrative build logs only at feature level: `Main quests authored: 16/33; branching conditions passing: 82%; dialogue data compiled`, not secret quest titles.
7. The player does not want to approve story spoilers. Treat this bible as delegated narrative direction; ask only when a genuinely gameplay-relevant choice cannot be inferred.

# 16. Data Design for Claude Code

Recommended structures (adapt to existing codebase):


- `narrative/private/bible.md` — this private source.

- `data/story/quests/*.json` — authored quest nodes and staged objectives, with IDs and localization keys.

- `data/story/dialogue/*.json` — dialogue trees with response predicates and action effects.

- `data/story/cases/*.json` — evidence, testimony, rumor links, contradictions and hypotheses.

- `data/story/endings/*.json` — policy eligibility rules and regional epilogue flags.

- `data/story/region_index.json` — region bounds, climate, music palettes, travel links and quest sites.

Sample quest object (structural, noncanonical exact schema):


Save compatibility: persistent UUIDs for NPCs, namespaced quest keys, migrations for later content versions, idempotent world state changes and case board evidence, data validation at startup. Never add a one-off mission script that bypasses the central persistence architecture.

Testing matrix:
- Branches completed in all 6 orders, 2-of-3 reconciliation, late third-branch return.
- Witness protected vs public, repair priority A vs B, all endings plus emergency fallback.
- Save/reload mid-dialogue, mid-investigation, mid-dungeon, post-world-change.
- Region unloaded/reloaded while NPC routines update abstractly.
- Manual save/autosave and respawn checkpoint without duplication or quest corruption.
- Optional guidance on/off; low visual effects; accessible puzzle clues.
- No required quest can deadlock because an NPC goes to sleep, dies, falls off terrain, or is stuck in navigation.
- Build can be installed alongside approved dependency mods in exactly Minecraft 1.21.1 Fabric.
